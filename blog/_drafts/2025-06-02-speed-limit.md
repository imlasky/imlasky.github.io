I’ve been thinking about Derek Sivers’ essay [There’s no speed limit](https://sive.rs/kimo) and have been struggling in my own life to come to grips with what this means in practice. 

While I agree, I can excel as fast as I want to and don’t have to be on a path, for some reason it feels like life has this speed limit on me anyways. 

Perhaps it’s because I’m trying to juggle many things at once, don’t have discipline, or a combination, but I can’t seem to shake this feeling that I can’t seem to get ahead and get to the place I need to be. Want to be. 

Somehow I find myself almost daily feeling like I haven’t accomplished anything. Yes, I feel lucky that I’m able to move around the way I can, but I feel tethered. Free, but in a walled garden. 

My ideas for products feel…lacking? Knocking around my echo chamber of reading hacker news, being remote, and unable to break out of my bubble, I’ve found myself stagnating mentally, even degrading. Thinking of solutions to problems that don’t exist. Wasting my energy building things that don’t matter for people and myself who won’t use them. 

I don’t want to surrender to the idea of being a nihilist or that the world is set up for me to remain in my position, but there’s days like today where I wonder “can I push through and prosper?”  

How do I find, acknowledge, and communicate with people with more interesting problems where I feel I’ve insulated most from myself? 

My dreams feel like they’ve dwindled. And I’m unhappy about it. 

The speed limit is true, but if there are multiple directions in which to expend your speed, does it mean I move nowhere? Fostering a life with my partner, learning Korean, exploring the world, building Daily Tokki, going back to learning violin, work, maintaining my weight loss — if they all pull in different directions at different speeds, am I doomed to remain where I am? Stuck?
